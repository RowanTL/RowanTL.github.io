## Overview

This is Problem 5.5 in Tu's Manifolds book. It asks to prove this proposition.

**Proposition 5.18 (An atlas for a product manifold).** *If $\{(U_\alpha, \phi_\alpha)\}$ and $\{(V_i, \psi_i)\}$ are $C^\infty$ atlases for the manifolds $M$ and $N$ of dimensions $m$ and $n$, respectively, then the collection*

$$\{(U_\alpha \times V_i,\ \phi_\alpha \times \psi_i : U_\alpha \times V_i \to \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n)\}$$

*of charts is a $C^\infty$ atlas on $M \times N$. Therefore, $M \times N$ is a $C^\infty$ manifold of dimension $m + n$.*

## Proof

It suffices to prove the statement, $$\{(U_\alpha, \phi_\alpha)\}$$ and $$\{(V_i, \psi_i)\}$$ are $$C^\infty$$ atlases for the manifolds $M$ and $N$ of dimensions $m$ and $n$, respectively, then the collection

$$\{(U_\alpha \times V_i,\ \phi_\alpha \times \psi_i : U_\alpha \times V_i \to \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n)\} = \mathfrak{A}$$

of charts is a $C^\infty$ atlas on $M \times N$.

As a roadmap, it suffices to show each $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ are homeomorphisms and pairwise $$C^\infty$$ compatible. Also, it must be shown that $$M \times N$$ has a cover of open sets found in $$\mathfrak{A}$$.

### Homeomorphism Proof

To show each $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is a homeomorphism, it suffices to show $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is injective, surjective, and continuous.

To prove injectivity, suppose $$p_1, p_2 \in U_\alpha$$ and $$q_1, q_2 \in V_i$$. Because $$\phi_\alpha$$ and $$\psi_i$$ are individually injective, we know if $$p_1 \ne p_2$$, then $$\phi_\alpha(p_1) \ne \phi_\alpha(p_2)$$, and if $$q_1 \ne q_2$$, then $$\psi_i(q_1) \ne \psi_i(q_2)$$. Next, observe

$$
\begin{aligned}
    (\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)(p_1, q_1) &= (\phi_\alpha(p_1), \psi_i(q_1)) \\
    (\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)(p_1, q_2) &= (\phi_\alpha(p_1), \psi_i(q_2)) \\
    (\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)(p_2, q_1) &= (\phi_\alpha(p_2), \psi_i(q_1)) \\
    (\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)(p_2, q_2) &= (\phi_\alpha(p_2), \psi_i(q_2))
\end{aligned}
$$

Because $$\phi_\alpha$$ and $$\psi_i$$ are injective, none of the four cases above are equal, showing $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is injective.

To show surjectivity, suppose $$(x, y) \in \phi_\alpha(U_\alpha) \times \psi_i(V_i)$$. Because $$\phi_\alpha$$ and $$\psi_i$$ are homeomorphisms, there exists $$p \in U_\alpha$$ and $$q \in V_i$$ such that $$\phi_\alpha(p) = x$$ and $$\psi_i(q) = y$$. Thus, $$(\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)(p, q) = (x, y)$$, showing $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is surjective.

With $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ being injective and surjective, it is bijective.

To show $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is continuous, it suffices to show for each open subset $$D \times E \subset \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n$$, the preimage $$(\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)^{-1}(D \times E)$$ is open in $$U_\alpha \times V_i$$.

Suppose $$D \times E \subset \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n$$ is open. Because $$\phi_\alpha$$ and $$\psi_i$$ are continuous, observe that $$\phi_\alpha^{-1}(D)$$ is open in $$U_\alpha$$ and $$\psi_i^{-1}(E)$$ is open in $$V_i$$. Next, we may build a basis element, namely $$(\phi_\alpha^{-1}(D), \psi_i^{-1}(E))$$, in $$U_\alpha \times V_i$$. Because basis elements are open, the element $$(\phi_\alpha^{-1}(D), \psi_i^{-1}(E)) = (\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)^{-1}(D \times E)$$ and is open in $$U_\alpha \times V_i$$. This proves $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is continuous. 

It remains to show $$(\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)^{-1} : \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow U_\alpha \times V_i$$ is continuous. Because $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is a bijection, we know an inverse function $$(\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)^{-1}$$ exists. It suffices to show for each open subset $$G \times H \subset U_\alpha \times V_i$$, the set $$(\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)(G \times H)$$ is open in $$\mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n$$.

Suppose $$G \times H \subset U_\alpha \times V_i$$ is open. Because $$\phi_\alpha^{-1}$$ and $$\psi_i^{-1}$$ are continuous, observe that $$\phi_\alpha(G)$$ is open in $$\mathbb{R}^m$$ and $$\psi_i(H)$$ is open in $$\mathbb{R}^n$$. Next, build a basis element, specifically $$(\phi_\alpha(G), \psi_i(H)) \subset \mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n$$. Since basis elements are open, the element $$(\phi_\alpha(G), \psi_i(H)) = (\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)(G \times H)$$ and is open in $$\mathbb{R}^m \times \mathbb{R}^n$$, proving $$(\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i)^{-1}$$ is continuous.

This concludes the section in proving $$\phi_\alpha \times \psi_i$$ is a homeomorphism.

### $$U_\alpha \times V_I$$ Covering

Because $$\{(U_\alpha \times V_i)\}$$ forms a basis for $$M \times N$$, the union of all $$U_\alpha \times V_i$$ forms a covering of\
$$M \times N$$.

### Pairwise $$C^\infty$$-compatible charts of $$M \times N$$

To demonstrate pairwise compatibility of all charts in $$M \times N$$, suppose $$(U_1 \times V_1, \phi_1 \times \psi_1), (U_2 \times V_2, \phi_2 \times \psi_2) \in \mathfrak{A}$$. It suffices to show

$$
    (\phi_1 \times \psi_1) \circ (\phi_2 \times \psi_2)^{-1} : (\phi_2 \times \psi_2)(U_1 \times V_1 \cap U_2 \times V_2) \rightarrow (\phi_1 \times \psi_1)(U_1 \times V_1 \cap U_2 \times V_2)
$$

and

$$
    (\phi_2 \times \psi_2) \circ (\phi_1 \times \psi_1)^{-1} : (\phi_1 \times \psi_1)(U_1 \times V_1 \cap U_2 \times V_2) \rightarrow (\phi_2 \times \psi_2)(U_1 \times V_1 \cap U_2 \times V_2)
$$

are $$C^\infty$$. Note if $$U_1 \times V_1 \cap U_2 \times V_2 = \emptyset$$, then the functions above are automatically $$C^\infty$$.
